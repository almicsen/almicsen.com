# Almicsen Design Direction

## Core Feel

Almicsen should feel dark-mode-first, cinematic, personal, and polished. The site should have enough restraint to feel business-safe, but enough personality to avoid looking like a default portfolio template.

The visual system should balance:

- A dark cinematic base.
- Apple-like premium spacing, rhythm, and restraint.
- Google-like usability, clarity, and obvious navigation.
- Futuristic glass/3D accents used sparingly.
- Nostalgic internet details that feel intentional, not chaotic.

## Identity

The primary public identity is almicsen.

Alan Michaelsen is the secondary identity and should only appear in About, business-safe, resume-style, or admin metadata contexts unless that direction changes later.

Do not use owner photos by default.

Do not invent personal details, exact location, contact information, or biographical stories.

## Copy and Tone

The tone should be personal, sharp, slightly funny, and still polished enough for professional readers.

Homepage hero line:

"I build creative digital things, but this site ain't one of them."

Supporting line:

"Projects, writing, experiments, and the occasional questionable Easter egg."

Avoid generic AI-template phrases, fake startup energy, and vague claims. The site should not say things like "unlock your potential," "transform your workflow," or "seamless experience" unless there is a very specific reason.

## Layout Principles

- Keep the interface scannable.
- Use clear page hierarchy.
- Keep navigation predictable.
- Use generous spacing on public pages.
- Use denser, practical layouts in admin surfaces.
- Avoid clutter, fake metrics, and decorative sections that do not serve content.

## Visual Rules

- No generic stock-photo filler.
- No owner photos by default.
- No fake testimonials.
- No fake corporate analytics.
- No excessive gradients.
- No obvious AI-generated portfolio look.
- No random decorative blobs or meaningless visual noise.
- Use glass/3D touches as accents, not the whole design language.

## Content Priorities

Public pages should foreground real work, writing, experiments, and shop links as they become available. Empty states should be honest and useful instead of pretending the platform is already full.

Friend-only features and Easter eggs should feel charming and controlled. They should not overpower the business-safe side of the site.
